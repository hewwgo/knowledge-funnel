Before you start

In this study you will read pairs of short texts. Each text is an answer that an AI assistant gave when someone asked it for an idea (a story idea, a product idea, a research idea, and so on). For each pair, you judge whether the two answers express the same idea.

  • About 60 minutes for about 32 pairs, split into three parts with a pause between them. Your progress is saved after every answer.
  • Please use a desktop or laptop with a wide browser window so both texts fit side by side.
  • The study starts with a short instruction page and three practice questions that check you have understood the task.
  • We store your judgments, the time you take per item, your progress through the study (including practice attempts), and your Prolific ID and session ID. We do not collect any other personal data. The judgments are used in academic research and may be published in anonymized form.
  • Participation is voluntary. You can stop at any time by returning the submission on Prolific.
  • Questions: the researcher via Prolific messages

How to judge a pair

You will see two answers to the same request, written for two different people. Ask yourself:

Do these two answers carry the same core idea?

You are rating the idea, not the writing. The core idea is what a person would take away and build on: the premise of the story, the product and what it does, the intervention and whom it serves. It is not the title, the wording, the length, the structure, or the quality.

The answers can be long. You do not need to read every word; read enough to know what idea each answer offers.

  1. Different ideas. Distinct ideas. Sharing a format, a tone, or a general topic does not make two answers related.
  2. Related. Clearly overlapping territory, but the central concept differs. A person could build two different things from them.
  3. Same idea, different execution. The same central concept, developed differently: other details, names, characters, setting, or framing.
  4. Same idea, same execution. The two answers would read as the same pitch: same concept and largely the same specifics.
4: two answers both propose an app that scans receipts to track spending, with the same features and flow.
3: an app that scans receipts to track spending, and an app that photographs receipts to categorize expenses. Same core idea, different details.
2: a receipt-scanning budget app, and an app for splitting expenses between roommates. Related territory, different concept.
1: a receipt-scanning budget app, and a meditation timer.

Some answers are a list of several alternative ideas. Judge the whole list, as what the person received. Two lists count as the same (3 or 4) only if they offer substantially the same set of ideas. If they share just some items, choose 2. If a single idea appears as one item in the other answer's list, also choose 2. A numbered list of steps or sections inside one idea is still one idea.
